00 / CONCEPT

FOOD on our table affects the planet.

Around 1/3 of global greenhouse-gas emissions comes from the food industry.

The Warming Table is a mixed-reality experience in which players work together to make food choices while balancing survival and environmental responsibility.

The table represents our daily grocery choices — creating a direct metaphor between the food placed on our tables and the impact placed on our planet.

02 / GAMEPLAY

Survive the week.

Survive the week. Preserve the island. Waste nothing.

Players are invited to spend a week on a beautiful, biodiverse island. Their task is to survive while protecting the natural world around them.

The catch is that the water rises as their carbon footprint increases. Every item of food has an expiry date, and players must decide whether to eat, freeze, share or waste it before time runs out.

They cannot consume everything at once, so survival depends on careful planning, communication and resource management. Rather than presenting climate data as an abstract statistic, the experience makes the consequences of collective decisions visible in real time.

03 / MULTIPLAYER

Four players. One ecosystem.

Four people sit at the table and take on four different roles. Each player can see and do something the others cannot, turning climate responsibility into a collective challenge that can only be solved through communication and teamwork.

PLAYER 01

Food Analyst

Can see the carbon footprint of every food item and reveal its environmental cost to the group.

PLAYER 02

Food Manager

Can pause expiration by freezing food, but only three items can be frozen at any one time.

PLAYER 03

Food Observer

Can see the expiration countdown for every item and warn the group before food becomes waste.

PLAYER 04

Dietitian

Can see every player's health bar and wellbeing status, helping the group balance survival and nutrition.

04 / DEVELOPMENT + TEAM

Built across disciplines.

Four people. Multiple disciplines. One shared vision.

The project was developed by a team of four in response to a client brief from UAL and UNIT9 as part of our curricular work. I took on the role of project lead, guiding the work from research and concept development through to the functional mixed-reality prototype.

Following submission, the project was taken forward for further development and put forward for the Innovate UK Immersive Tech Awards. The interdisciplinary process brought together creative direction, game design, XR development, real-time interaction and 3D animation.
